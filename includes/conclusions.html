Summary of Work to Date and Key Contributions

Key Contributions

Griddy Gibbs Calibration

  1. By using a discrete grid of possible points, precomputation can be leveraged to greatly improve the computational expense of MCMC
  2. The proposed grid selection approach allows efficient selection and evaluation of the discrete grid to produce comparable posterior estimates to traditional continuous MCMC

Key Contributions

Griddy Gibbs Calibration

  1. Integrating the discrepancy term improves stability and convergence of MCMC
    • Discrepancy is not directly estimated in the mean vector
    • The discrepancy is still accounted for in the covariance matrix

Key Contributions

Sensor Optimization

  1. One-at-a-time optimization approach resolves issues with D-optimality approaches in literature
    • Determinant of Fisher information matrix can lead to multiple optima
    • D-optimality approach is computationally infeasible for computationally expensive computer models

Key Contributions

Sensor Optimization

  1. Mesh mapping approach easily handles optimization over complicated domains
    • Map any arbitrary proposed solution from $\mathbb{R}^n$ to the piston's FE mesh
  2. Efficient exploration of the domain by genetic algorithm

Key Contributions

Sensor Optimization

  1. Two-stage approach is robust to uncertainty
    • First stage considers many possible values of the unknown "truth"
    • Second stage identifies the best sensor location, considering the uncertainty of the unknown "truth"

Key Contributions

Fatigue Data Analysis

  1. Flexible and implicit representation of $S$-$N$ and cumulative damage models with a splines-based hazard model
    • Achieves good estimates of the underlying $S$-$N$ curve for data with both positive and negative stress steps
    • No need to explicitly specify the form of $S$-$N$ and cumulative damage model, like is needed with traditional modeling approaches

Key Contributions

Fatigue Data Analysis

  1. Proposed optimal experiment design approach results in tests that are far more informative than traditional test designs
    • Improved estimation with far fewer tests
    • Useful information obtained from each new test design

Key Contributions

Fatigue Data Analysis

  1. Allowing negative stress steps in test designs can reduce the time needed to test specimens
    • Exhaust a portion of damage at a high stress
    • Reduce the stress and observe failure at a low stress in fewer cycles
    • A good representation of the cumulative damage mechanism is necessary
    • Explicit cumulative damage models in traditional modeling approaches are sensitive to mis-specification with negative stress steps

Key Contributions

Fatigue Data Analysis

  1. Pairing the optimal experiment design with the splines-based hazard model results in robust estimation of arbitrary $S$-$N$ curves in an efficient manner
    • The proposed hazard model is robust to the $S$-$N$ curve and cumulative damage model used to generate the data
    • The optimal experiment design, allowing negative stress steps, achieves efficient estimation
