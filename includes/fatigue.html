Likelihood

Approach

Regression Splines

Solution: use M/I splines
  • M splines are a highly flexible non-parametric method for estimating unknown functions
  • They have corresponding integrals called I splines that are monotonically increasing
  • Transitioning between M and I splines is simply a change of basis
  • Extremely efficient for calculations since the M and I bases can be precomputed
  • Only requires estimation of basis coefficients $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m$

Modeling Approach

  • Model the baseline hazard function, $\lambda_0(n)$, with M splines
    • Consequently, the cumulative baseline hazard function, $\Lambda_0(n)$, is modeled by the corresponding I splines
  • Model the risk function, $g[s(n)]$, with a separate set of M splines

Modeling Approach

$$\footnotesize \begin{align*} \lambda_0(n) =& \sum_{a=1}^{m_1} M_{a,1}(n) \gamma_{a}\\ \Lambda_0(n) =& \sum_{a=1}^{m_1} I_{a,1}(n) \gamma_{a}\\ g[s(n)] =& \sum_{a=1}^{m_2} M_{a,2}[s(n)] \beta_{a} \end{align*}$$

Optimal Experiment Design

Sequential Bayesian optimal experiment design
After a test, this new data is used to update the model's posterior distributions. The updated model is then used to select the next point.

Objective Function

Shannon entropy $$H(Y) = -\sum_Y \pi(y) \log \pi(y)$$
Measures the average amount of uncertainty in the variable $Y$
We can construct our objective function based on entropy to select the design point $\mathbf{d}$ that maximizes the reduction in entropy for the next test point

Objective Function

This is equivalent to the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the posterior distributions at current state $m$ and future state $m+1$ $$\small \begin{align*} O^*(\mathbf{d}) = & D_{\textrm{KL}}\Big([\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\beta}|\mathbf{N}^m,\mathbf{s}(n)]\Big|\Big|\\ & [\boldsymbol{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\beta}|\mathbf{N}^{m+1},\mathbf{s}(n),\mathbf{d}]\Big) \end{align*} $$

Conclusions

  1. The proposed hazard model achieves good estimates of the $S$-$N$ curve without needing to specify a form of the $S$-$N$ curve or cumulative damage model
    • Comparable to a properly-specified traditional model when using typical data
  2. The proposed hazard model is not sensitive to positive v.s. negative stress steps in the data set
    • Traditional modeling approaches are sensitive due to their parametric cumulative damage specification

Conclusions

  1. The proposed optimal experiment design procedure allows the hazard model to achieve good estimation of the $S$-$N$ curve efficiently
    • Only a few new test points are needed
    • Each new test point is meaningful, compared to traditional test designs
  2. The proposed hazard model is robust to the form of the data generating model
    • Across all considered $S$-$N$ and cumulative damage models, the proposed hazard model was able to effectively represent the underlying $S$-$N$ curve well

Conclusions

  1. The efficiency of the optimal experiment design is based on the allowance of tests with negative stress steps
  2. Analyzing data with negative stress steps requires good knowledge of the cumulative damage mechanism
    • This is only works because of how flexible and robust the proposed splines-based hazard model is
